    Guide

    Sources of sound

    Sing
    Analyzes your own voice through the microphone.
    Synthesize
    Plays an artificial vowel whose pitch, formants and volume you choose, so you can hear and see how each one shapes the sound.
    Listen
    Plays a short aria excerpt for each voice type and analyzes the singer.

    All analysis happens on your device. Your voice is never uploaded.

    Pitch

    The large letter names the nearest note, and its colour tells you how well you are tuned to it. Tuning is judged on your average pitch over about half a second.

    Green
    In tune: within 10 cents of the note.
    Orange
    Slightly sharp or flat: within 30 cents.
    Grey
    Between notes.

    A cent is one hundredth of a semitone.

    Vibrato

    Rate
    How many times per second the pitch rises and falls, in hertz (Hz). The shaded band, 5.5–6.7 Hz, is the range measured in ten celebrated opera singers (Prame 1994). The tick at 6 Hz marks the rate listeners found most appropriate (Anand et al. 2012).
    Extent
    How far the pitch swings above and below the note, in cents. Professional singers have been measured between about ±34 and ±123 cents, with an average of ±71 (Prame 1997). Listeners judged very wide vibratos less appropriate.

    Vibrato naturally quickens toward the end of a note.

    Formants

    A formant is a resonance of the vocal tract: the throat, mouth and lips. By shaping the vocal tract, singers move their formants and so change both the vowel and the colour of the voice.

    F0
    Not a formant but the fundamental frequency: the pitch you sing.
    F1
    Rises as the jaw opens. It is low in [i] and [u] and high in [a].
    F2
    Rises as the tongue moves forward. It is high in [i] and low in [u].
    F3
    Falls as the lips round.

    The voice itself sounds only at its harmonics, the whole-number multiples of F0. A formant therefore makes itself heard only when a harmonic lies on or near it, which it then strengthens.

    F3 · ring

    Ring, also called the singer's formant or squillo (Italian for "ringing, clarion"), is the bright, carrying quality that lets a trained voice be heard over an orchestra. It arises when F3, F4 and F5 cluster into a single strong peak between about 2.5 and 3.2 kHz (Sundberg 1974). The ear is especially sensitive in this region, and the orchestra is comparatively quiet there, so energy placed here carries. The green zone on the bar marks this region.

    The RING meter compares your level near 3 kHz with what an ordinary speaking voice would produce on the same vowel (Sundberg 1995).

    0 dB
    Like speech.
    +6 dB or more
    A singer's formant is present.

    The reading rises somewhat as you sing louder, and it is less reliable on high notes, where the harmonics are widely spaced. In Detail view the bar also shows your spectrum from 2 to 4 kHz, turned on its side: the filled shape is your voice, and the dotted line is the speech-like reference.

    Vowel · F1 × F2

    The vowel map is laid out like the IPA vowel chart, with front vowels on the left and close vowels at the top. The circles show the seven Italian vowels for your voice range, or your own vowels once you have calibrated them.

    Resonance tuning

    As they sing higher, trained singers adjust their resonances so that a harmonic stays on a formant. High and low voices do this differently:

    • High voices sing notes that rise above F1. Once the note reaches F1, they raise F1 with it, so the note itself stays on the resonance. Many also raise F2 to meet the second harmonic (twice the note).
    • Low voices sing notes that stay below F1. Near the top of their range, the second harmonic reaches F1 instead, and they tune F1 to it.

    The vowel circles shift in the same way as you go higher. The dotted lines H1, H2 and H3 mark the first three harmonics.

    Input

    Shows how strong the microphone signal is, in decibels below the loudest level the device can record. It is not a measure of how loud you sing in the room. CLIP means the signal has hit that limit: the sound is distorted, so readings pause until it clears. Turn down the microphone gain or step back.

    声音来源

    演唱
    通过麦克风分析你自己的声音。
    合成
    播放一个人工元音，音高、共振峰和音量由你选择，让你听到并看到每一项如何塑造声音。
    聆听
    为每个声部播放一段咏叹调片段，并分析其中的歌手。

    所有分析都在你的设备上进行，你的声音不会上传。

    音高

    大字母是最接近的音名，颜色表示你唱得有多准。音准依据约半秒内的平均音高判断。

    绿色
    音准：与该音相差 10 音分以内。
    橙色
    略高或略低：相差 30 音分以内。
    灰色
    介于两个音之间。

    1 音分是半音的百分之一。

    颤音

    速率
    音高每秒起伏的次数，单位为赫兹（Hz）。阴影带 5.5–6.7 Hz 是在十位著名歌剧演唱家身上测得的范围（Prame 1994）。6 Hz 处的刻度是听众认为最合适的速率（Anand 等 2012）。
    幅度
    音高在本音上下摆动的幅度，以音分计。职业歌手的测量值约在 ±34 至 ±123 音分之间，平均 ±71（Prame 1997）。听众认为过宽的颤音不太合适。

    颤音在音的结尾处会自然加快。

    共振峰

    共振峰是声道（咽喉、口腔和双唇）的共振。歌手通过改变声道的形状来移动共振峰，从而同时改变元音和音色。

    F0
    不是共振峰，而是基频，即你唱的音高。
    F1
    下颌张开时升高。[i] 和 [u] 的 F1 低，[a] 的 F1 高。
    F2
    舌位前移时升高。[i] 的 F2 高，[u] 的 F2 低。
    F3
    双唇收圆时降低。

    嗓音本身只在谐波上发声，谐波即 F0 的整数倍。因此，只有当某个谐波落在共振峰上或附近时，这个共振峰才会被听到，并使该谐波增强。

    F3 · 共鸣

    共鸣（ring），又称歌手共振峰或 squillo（意大利语，意为"嘹亮、号角般"），是训练有素的嗓音明亮、穿透力强、能越过乐队被听见的特质。它出现在 F3、F4、F5 聚集成约 2.5–3.2 kHz 之间的一个强峰时（Sundberg 1974）。人耳对这一频段格外敏感，而乐队在这里相对较弱，所以放在这里的能量传得远。竖条上的绿色区域标出这一频段。

    共鸣表把你在 3 kHz 附近的能量，与普通说话声在同一元音上的能量相比较（Sundberg 1995）。

    0 dB
    与说话相同。
    +6 dB 及以上
    具有歌手共振峰。

    唱得越响，读数会略有升高；在高音上谐波间距很大，读数不太可靠。在详细视图中，竖条还会横向显示你 2–4 kHz 的频谱：填充形状是你的声音，虚线是说话声的参考。

    元音 · F1 × F2

    元音图的布局与国际音标元音图相同：前元音在左，闭元音在上。圆圈是你所选音域的七个意大利语元音；校准之后则是你自己的元音。

    共振调谐

    唱高时，训练有素的歌手会调整共振，让某个谐波始终落在共振峰上。高声部和低声部的做法不同：

    • 高声部唱的音会超过 F1。音高一旦到达 F1，她们就让 F1 随音高一起上升，使这个音本身始终落在共振上。许多人还会把 F2 提高到第二谐波（音高的两倍）。
    • 低声部唱的音一直低于 F1。在音域顶端，到达 F1 的是第二谐波，于是他们把 F1 调到第二谐波上。

    你唱高时，元音圆圈也会以同样方式移动。虚线 H1、H2、H3 标出前三个谐波。

    输入

    显示麦克风信号的强度，单位是低于设备可录制最大电平的分贝数。它并不表示你在房间里唱得有多响。CLIP 表示信号已到达这个上限：声音失真，读数暂停，直到恢复正常。请调低麦克风增益或离远一些。
